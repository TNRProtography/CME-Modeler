Everything it does

Three pages. One complete picture.

The Forecast page answers "should I go outside tonight?". The Solar Activity page shows you what the Sun is doing right now. The CME Visualization page shows you what's already on its way. Here's what's inside each of them.

Page one

The Forecast page

The page you'll open ninety percent of the time. It exists to answer one question honestly: can you see the aurora from where you are, right now, and is it worth going out?

Simple View — no jargon at all

One big percentage, an icon, a status word and a single sentence telling you what that actually means for your eyes and your camera. Underneath, four time slots — Now, 15 min, 30 min and 60 min — each written in plain English like "conditions are building" rather than a number you have to interpret.

Below that: the live sightings map, a cloud-cover overlay from Windy, and live cameras. Everything you need to make the call, nothing you need to decode.

forecast-advanced-view.png Advanced View showing the gauge row (Bz, Bt, speed, density, hemispheric power, moon) and at least one chart below.

Advanced View — every number, every chart

  • Live gauges: IMF Bz and Bt, solar wind speed, density, hemispheric power, and moon illumination.
  • IMF chart with Bx and By toggleable, and each data point labelled with which spacecraft it came from.
  • IMF clock angle dial — watch the field vector rotate; six o'clock is maximum coupling.
  • Solar wind charts: speed, density and temperature over 24 hours.
  • NZ magnetometer: the Eyrewell dH trace — the ground truth for New Zealand.
  • Hemispheric power chart from the GOES satellites.
  • Disturbance index (Dst) with storm-level thresholds.
  • Moon arc chart — rise, set and illumination through the night.
  • Substorm panel: status ladder, P30/P60 probabilities, Newell coupling and bay onset detection.
📈

Forecast trend chart

Your score over the past 24–48 hours, with upcoming cloud cover layered on top. Sunset, moonrise and moonset are drawn as vertical lines, and detected substorms are marked with timestamps. Hover any point for the exact score, Bz, speed and density at that moment.

🗺️

Live sightings map

Reports from other people in New Zealand, pinned where they are: naked eye, phone camera, DSLR, nothing, or clouded out. Submit your own with one tap. Recent nearby reports feed directly into the "Now" slot of the forecast — human eyes confirming what the satellites suggest.

📷

Seven live cameras

Oban, Queenstown, Twizel, Taylors Mistake, Opiki, Rangitikei and New Plymouth. Check whether the sky is actually clear somewhere before you commit to driving there — and occasionally catch the aurora on camera before you've left the house.

Page two

The Solar Activity page

What the Sun is doing at this moment. This is where tomorrow night's aurora starts — and where you spot it two or three days before anyone posts about it.

⚡

X-ray flux & flares

24 hours of live GOES X-ray flux with the B, C, M and X class thresholds marked. Every detected flare is clickable: begin, peak and end times in NZ time, source location on the disk, active region number, and a direct link to NASA's record.

If that flare launched a CME, one button takes you straight to that CME in the 3D visualisation — you see the flare, then you watch what it threw at us.

🌑

Active sunspot tracker

The current sunspot regions mapped onto live SDO/HMI solar imagery, in three modes: colourised magnetogram, black-and-white magnetogram, and intensitygram. Swipe to cycle, tap to go fullscreen and zoom right in.

Each region carries its official classification, spot count, magnetic class, and NOAA's M-class, X-class and proton flare probabilities — so you know which region to watch.

⚛️

Proton flux

The ≥10, ≥50 and ≥100 MeV channels with the S-scale event threshold marked at 10 pfu. A proton event is both an aurora signal and a radiation-storm signal.

🌕

SUVI & CCOR-1 coronagraph

The latest extreme-ultraviolet image of the corona, plus CCOR-1 coronagraph video — where you can literally watch a CME billow off the Sun. Everything taps to fullscreen.

Page three · World first

The CME Visualization

A real-time, interactive 3D model of the inner solar system with every catalogued coronal mass ejection travelling through it. Nothing else publicly available does this.

cme-3d-wide.png Full-width capture of the 3D scene — Sun, Earth, CME cloud, planet labels and the controls panel visible.
🌌

Real physics, not animation

CMEs are propagated with the drag-based model of Vršnak et al. (2013) — a fast CME ploughing into slower ambient wind genuinely decelerates, and the drag is calibrated against the solar wind speed measured at L1 right now.

🎨

Speed you can read at a glance

CME colour tracks current speed: grey near ambient wind speed, through yellow at 500 km/s, orange at 800, red-orange at 1000, purple at 1800, and pink for the extreme 2500 km/s+ events.

⏰

Scrub through time

Play, pause, step or drag through seven days of history plus your chosen forecast window. A marker shows "now" — everything to the right of it is forecast.

📊

Simulated impact forecast

A seven-day chart of the speed and density expected to arrive at Earth, built from the decelerated arrival speeds of everything currently in flight — including the density spike that leads a high-speed stream by about 18 hours.

🌑

Live coronal hole detection

The SUVI solar image is analysed in your browser to find coronal holes, which are then drawn on the disk with Parker spiral arms projecting their high-speed stream out to 1.65 AU — well past Earth.

🔭

Explore it properly

Rotate, pan and zoom. Toggle the inner planets, labels, Earth's Moon and the L1 point. Switch between top-down and side views. Click an individual CME particle cloud to read its full details.

Notifications

Ten alert categories, all opt-in

Turn on only what you want. The three visibility tiers use your GPS and the real geometry of the auroral oval; everything else fires on measured events at the satellites.

Built to not annoy you. Visibility alerts only escalate — once you've had the camera-tier alert you won't get it again for that night unless conditions improve to the next tier. Shock alerts have a four-hour cooldown, each visibility tier has a two-hour per-person cooldown, and the overnight summary is capped at one per calendar day.

And a detail that matters if you care about privacy: notification payloads are end-to-end encrypted to your device. The entire Web Push stack — VAPID signing, key agreement, AES-128-GCM encryption — was written from scratch rather than handed to a third-party push service. Nobody in the middle, including us, can read the contents of your alerts.

Small things you'll notice after a week

🌙

Night mode

A red-shifted display mode so checking the app at a dark site doesn't destroy the dark adaptation you spent twenty minutes building.

🔗

Shareable deep links

Navigation lives in the URL. Send someone a link straight to a specific CME in the 3D scene and it opens exactly there.

📚

Built-in tutorials

A first-visit walkthrough, per-page tutorials, an FAQ, and full technical documentation inside the app — every formula, every data source, nothing hidden.

📊

Honest data freshness

Every panel shows when its data last updated, and flags it when a source goes stale — so you're never reading an hour-old number thinking it's live.

🎮

Solar Surfer

There's a little game hidden in there for cloudy nights. Because sometimes the sky is closed and you still want to be doing something space-related.

🔊

Sitewide announcements

When something genuinely big is happening, a banner goes up in the app. No guessing whether the numbers are real.

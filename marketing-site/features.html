Page one

The Forecast page

The page you'll open ninety percent of the time. It exists to answer one question honestly. Can you see the aurora from where you are, right now, and is it worth going out?

Simple View, no jargon at all

One big percentage, a status word and a single sentence telling you what that actually means for your eyes and your camera. Underneath, four time slots: Now, 15 minutes, 30 minutes and 60 minutes, each written in plain English like "conditions are building" rather than a number you have to interpret.

Below that, the live sightings map, a cloud cover overlay from Windy, and the cameras. Everything you need to make the call, nothing you need to decode.

The app's own Advanced View panels, live

Advanced View, every number and every chart

  • Live gauges. IMF Bz and Bt, solar wind speed, density, hemispheric power, and moon illumination.
  • IMF chart with Bx and By toggleable, each point labelled with which spacecraft it came from.
  • IMF clock angle dial. Watch the field vector rotate. Six o'clock is maximum coupling.
  • Solar wind charts. Speed, density and temperature over 24 hours.
  • NZ magnetometer. The Eyrewell trace, which is the ground truth for this country.
  • Hemispheric power chart from the GOES satellites.
  • Disturbance index with storm level thresholds.
  • Moon arc chart. Rise, set and illumination through the night.
  • Substorm panel. Status ladder, 30 and 60 minute probabilities, coupling and bay onset detection.

The next three days, hour by hour

The app's own forecast, not a Kp number borrowed from somewhere else. Every hour for three days shows the aurora you could actually see from where you are: a full block for naked eye, two thirds for a phone camera, a third for a proper camera, with an eye, phone or camera marked on the hour.

It already allows for daylight, twilight and the Moon at that hour, and it marks when a coronal hole stream from the Coronal Hole Tracker or a CME from the CME Visualization is due to arrive, so you can see what is driving the good nights.

Forecast trend chart

Your score over the past 24 to 48 hours with upcoming cloud cover layered on top. Sunset, moonrise and moonset are drawn in as vertical lines, and detected substorms are marked with timestamps. Hover any point for the exact score, Bz, speed and density at that moment.

Live sightings map

Reports from other people around the country, pinned where they are. Naked eye, phone camera, DSLR, nothing, or clouded out. Submit your own with one tap. Recent nearby reports feed straight into the Now slot, which is human eyes confirming what the satellites are suggesting.

Seven live cameras

Oban, Queenstown, Twizel, Taylors Mistake, Opiki, Rangitikei and New Plymouth. Check whether the sky is actually clear somewhere before you commit to driving there, and occasionally catch the aurora on camera before you've left the house.

Page two

The Solar Activity page

What the Sun is doing at this moment. This is where tomorrow night's aurora starts, and where you spot it two or three days before anyone posts about it.

X-ray flux and flares

24 hours of live flux with the B, C, M and X class thresholds marked. Every detected flare is clickable: begin, peak and end times in NZ time, source location on the disk, active region number, and a direct link to NASA's record.

If that flare launched a CME, one button takes you straight to it in the 3D visualisation. You see the flare, then you watch what it threw at us.

Active sunspot tracker

The current sunspot regions mapped onto live solar imagery in three modes: colourised magnetogram, black and white magnetogram, and intensitygram. Swipe to cycle, tap to go fullscreen and zoom right in.

Each region carries its classification, spot count, magnetic class, and NOAA's M class, X class and proton flare probabilities, so you know which one to watch.

Coronal hole tracker

Every coronal hole on the disk, found live in the SUVI imagery, with its size, the speed of its stream, its magnetic polarity, and when that stream reaches Earth. Holes split by a thin bright sliver are treated as the one hole they are.

Each hole keeps its number for life, and its whole history is kept for 90 days: when it first appeared, how its size and stream speed changed, and when and why it went, whether it closed, merged into another or turned off the edge of the Sun.

Proton flux

The 10, 50 and 100 MeV channels with the event threshold marked. A proton event is both an aurora signal and a radiation storm signal.

Coronal imagery and coronagraph

The latest extreme ultraviolet image of the corona, plus coronagraph video where you can watch a CME billow off the Sun. Everything taps to fullscreen.

Below are the app's own Active Sunspot Tracker and Coronal Hole Tracker, running live: the current imagery of the Sun with every region and hole mapped onto it. Tap a region for its magnetic flux and area over the last few days, and how its spot count has changed in the last 24 hours. Tap a hole for when it appeared, how its size and stream speed have changed, and when it will reach us.

Page three

The CME Visualization

A real time, interactive 3D model of the inner solar system with every catalogued coronal mass ejection travelling through it. As far as we know, nothing else publicly available does this.

The 3D CME sceneThis runs the live model from the app. If it has not appeared, open it in the app itself.Open the app

Real physics, not animation

CMEs are propagated with a published drag based model, so a fast one ploughing into slower ambient wind genuinely decelerates. The drag is calibrated against the solar wind speed being measured at L1 right now.

Speed you can read at a glance

Colour tracks current speed. Grey near ambient wind, through yellow at 500 km/s, orange at 800, red orange at 1000, purple at 1800, and pink for the extreme 2500 km/s events.

Scrub through time

Play, pause, step or drag through seven days of history plus your chosen forecast window. A marker shows now, and everything to the right of it is forecast.

Simulated impact forecast

A seven day chart of the speed and density expected to arrive at Earth, built from the decelerated arrival speeds of everything currently in flight, including the density spike that leads a high speed stream by about 18 hours.

Coronal holes through time

Coronal holes are drawn on the Sun in their real shape, with Parker spiral arms carrying their streams out past Earth. Scrub the timeline and every hole is shown as it was at that moment: opening, growing, changing shape, and gone once it closed or turned away.

Explore it properly

Rotate, pan and zoom. Toggle the inner planets, labels, the Moon and the L1 point. Switch between top down and side views. Click a CME cloud to read its full details.

The details

Small things you'll notice after a week

Night mode

A red shifted display so checking the app at a dark site doesn't destroy the dark adaptation you spent twenty minutes building.

Shareable links

Navigation lives in the URL. Send someone a link straight to a specific CME in the 3D scene and it opens exactly there.

Built in tutorials

A first visit walkthrough, per page tutorials, an FAQ, and the full technical documentation inside the app. Every formula and every data source, nothing hidden.

Honest data freshness

Every panel shows when its data last updated and flags it when a source goes stale, so you're never reading an hour old number thinking it's live.

Storm Builder

A game for cloudy nights. You get a town and a time, and only the Sun end to work with: aim the eruption, pick its speed and twist its flux rope to put aurora over them.

Announcements

When something genuinely big is happening, a banner goes up in the app. No guessing whether the numbers are real.

Worth a look before you commit to the drive.

Free, no ads, made on the Coast. Open it in your browser, add it to your home screen, and stop missing nights you never knew were happening.
